**Research date:** 2026-07-28
**Company:** Alix (estate settlement), founded 2022 by Alexandra Mysoor (CEO) and Hugh Tamassia (CTO).
**Canonical domain:** `meetalix.com` — **not** `alixnow.com`. `alixnow.com` refused the connection

## Summary: which denominator to build on

**Use 570 executor-hours per estate as the denominator, and cite EstateExec, not Alix.**
It is the only hours-per-estate figure in this entire chain that has a published methodology
(n = 1,201 randomly-sampled US adults, ±3% error margin) attached to it. Alix's own numbers —
"900 hours", "600-900 hours", "600+ hours", "hundreds of hours" — appear on at least six different
company pages with **no source citation anywhere**, and the trail leads back to Mysoor's own single
estate: *"Little did I know, that simple list would consume 900 hours and over 18 months to complete"*
(meetalix.com/about-us). That is an n=1 personal anecdote that has been progressively restated as a
population average, including by Mysoor herself — *"it took me 18 months and 900 hours to complete,
**which is an average amount of time for a simple estate**"* (Business Insider, 2025-12-16). Journalists
now launder it back as *"industry estimates"* (InvestmentNews) with no industry study behind it.
Building on 900 means your whole impact model dies to one question: "where does 900 come from?"
Building on 570 survives that question. **If you must match Alix's marketing language, use their
published range verbatim — "600-900 hours" (go.meetalix.com/probate) — because its low end brackets
the real survey figure and a range cannot be attacked as a false point estimate.** Separately, note
that the single hardest, most checkable number Alix publishes is not an hours figure at all but its
price: **1% of estate assets, $9,000 minimum**. If you need a denominator that nobody can dispute,
use the fee.

### Is there a published hours-per-estate figure? Yes — but read the caveat

There **is** a published figure, repeatedly and prominently: **900 hours**. So a pitch does not need
to invent one. **But Alix has never published a source for it**, and the nearest thing to real
research (EstateExec, 570 hours) is ~37% lower. So:

> Any pitch that uses **900 hours** must attribute it as **"Alix's stated figure"** — it is a company
> marketing claim, not a research finding. Any pitch that presents 900 hours as an independent industry
> statistic is asserting something no published study supports.

## The 900-vs-570 gap, with the arithmetic shown

This is **my construction**, not anyone's published claim. Inputs and steps so you can disagree with a
specific step:

- Input 1: EstateExec survey, 570 hours average executor effort, n = 1,201, ±3% (C6/C7).
- Input 2: Alix's most-repeated figure, 900 hours (A1, A7, A11).
- Step: `900 / 570 = 1.579` → Alix's figure is **~58% higher** than the survey figure.
- Step: `(900 - 570) / 900 = 0.367` → **~37% of Alix's stated denominator is unsupported** by the only
  survey with a published methodology.
- Consequence for an impact model: an "hours saved" number built on 900 is **~58% larger** than the
  same model built on 570, purely from the denominator choice, before any efficacy assumption.

Second construction, on the savings claim — again mine, not Alix's:

- Input: Alix's own "95%" workload reduction (A15) applied to its own 900-hour denominator (A7).
- Step: `900 × 0.95 = 855 hours saved`, leaving `45 hours` of family effort.
- Cross-check against Alix's other published reduction figure, 85% (B7, 2024): `900 × 0.85 = 765 hours
  saved`, leaving `135 hours`.
- The two company figures imply family residual effort of **45 hours vs 135 hours — a 3× spread**.
  Alix publishes both without reconciling them. Pick one and say which, or the model is indefensible.

## Notes, discrepancies, and what is NOT published

**Alix's hours figure is internally inconsistent across its own pages.** In one place it is a point
estimate ("about 900 hours", A7), in another a range ("600-900 hours", A6), in another a floor
("600+ hours", A18), and in another a vague qualifier ("hundreds of hours", A8/A25). All four are
live on meetalix.com simultaneously.

**The duration figure is also inconsistent.** "12-18 months" (A1, A6, A23, A30) versus "9–18 months"
(A8) versus Alix's own resource page citing a Trust & Will study for "an average of 20 months" (A12).
The press release and the About page disagree with each other and with Alix's own cited source.

**The reduction percentage is inconsistent.** 85% in 2024 (B7) versus 95% in 2026 (A15). No page
explains the change. If you cite a reduction percentage, cite the date with it.

**The wealth-transfer market number moves between $84T, $90T, $100T and $124T** depending on the
venue and year (B2/C8 = $84T; C4 = $90T; B6 = $100T; A4/C2 = $124T). All four are attributed to Alix
or its investors within a 3-year window. Do not use this as a stable denominator.

**Provenance of "900 hours" — my reading of the trail, stated as inference, not as fact.** The
earliest occurrence I found is Mysoor's personal account of settling one estate over 18 months
(A9, B11). By December 2023 it had become "600-900 hours of effort" as a general claim (B1). By 2025
Mysoor states it as an average outright (B4). By July 2025 InvestmentNews reports it as "industry
estimates" (C1) and Acrew as "on average" (C3). **I could not find any study, survey, or dataset that
Alix cites for 900.** Forbes, writing about Alix in 2023, used the EstateExec number (nearly 600 hours,
C5) — suggesting the real research figure was in circulation around Alix from the start.
